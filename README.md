### Building is how I stay current

Deciding where a company bets on AI is my job. The fastest way I have found to form a real
opinion is to build with it myself: take whatever shipped last month, put it inside a product
that people actually pay for, and find out where it breaks. Reading the launch post tells you
what a model promises. Running it in production tells you what it costs.

My degree is in business computing, so the hands-on part is also simply the part I enjoy.

Three of these are live, with paying users, App Store reviews and support tickets. I am not a
professional engineer; I design the systems, build them with AI coding agents, and own them in
production, including the deploys, the incidents and the bills.

**Most of that code is private.** What lives here are documented case studies: the
architecture, the decisions behind it, and what broke.

### On voice

The most interesting thing I built this year is a voice agent that conducts a structured
interview and writes the answers into a profile while the conversation is still running. I
came at it three ways: a self-built stack on Gemini Live, ElevenLabs Conversational AI, and
OpenAI Realtime. The last two run in parallel on the same question plan, switchable per
session, each going through the whole candidate journey.

That left me with something fairly rare for someone on the commercial side: a measured opinion
about where conversational voice actually breaks, and what it takes to make it feel like one
conversation rather than a pipeline. The write-up is
**[here](https://github.com/tibor-stefan/gtm-guide)**.
